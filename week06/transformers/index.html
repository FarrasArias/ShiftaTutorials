Filters & Effects

So far we've generated sound (oscillators) and analyzed it (FFT). Now we'll process it: changing its spectral content and adding spatial effects. This is where synthesis starts sounding like production.

What Are Filters?

An audio filter selectively removes or attenuates certain frequencies from a sound. It's the sonic equivalent of a color filter on a camera lens: it lets some frequencies through while blocking others.

The three fundamental filter types:

  • Low-pass filter (LPF): passes frequencies below a cutoff frequency, attenuates frequencies above it. Makes sounds darker, warmer, muffled. Think of hearing music through a wall: the wall is a low-pass filter.
  • High-pass filter (HPF): passes frequencies above the cutoff, attenuates below. Removes bass, making sounds thinner and brighter. A telephone line is essentially a high-pass filter.
  • Band-pass filter (BPF): passes a band of frequencies around a center frequency, attenuates everything else. Like tuning a radio: you isolate one station (frequency band) from the noise.

The key parameters of any filter are:

  • Cutoff frequency: the frequency where the filter starts attenuating. For a low-pass at 1000 Hz, frequencies above 1000 Hz get quieter.
  • Resonance (Q): a boost at the cutoff frequency. High resonance creates a sharp peak: the filter "rings" at the cutoff. This is the classic synthesizer filter sweep sound.

p5.Filter

In p5.sound, filters are created with new p5.Filter(type) where type is 'lowpass', 'highpass', or 'bandpass':

The signal chain here is: oscillator → filter → speakers. We manually disconnect() the oscillator from the default output and connect() it to the filter instead. The filter's output goes to the speakers by default.

Move the mouse left and right while holding click to sweep the cutoff frequency. This is the "wah-wah" or "filter sweep" sound that's fundamental to electronic music. Move the mouse up for high resonance: you'll hear the filter start to "sing" at the cutoff frequency.

Signal Chains

Audio modules in p5.sound can be chained together just like physical audio equipment. Each module has an input and output, and we connect them with .connect() and .disconnect():

// Source → Filter → Output
osc.disconnect();
osc.connect(filter);
// filter output goes to speakers by default

// Source → Filter → Reverb → Output
osc.disconnect();
osc.connect(filter);
filter.disconnect();
filter.connect(reverb);

// Analyzing the final output
fft.setInput(reverb);

This modular approach: connecting processing blocks in series: is the fundamental architecture of every synthesizer and audio workstation. Each block transforms the signal, and the order matters: filtering before reverb sounds very different from reverb before filtering.

Delay

A delay effect records the incoming signal and plays it back after a specified time, creating echoes. The key parameters are:

  • Delay time: how long before the echo plays (in seconds)
  • Feedback: how much of the delayed signal is fed back into the input, creating repeating echoes. At 0, you get a single echo. At high values, echoes repeat many times, gradually fading.
open example in new window

delay.process(source, delayTime, feedback, filterFreq) connects the source to the delay and configures it in one call. The filterFreq parameter applies a low-pass filter to the delayed signal: this mimics how real echoes lose high-frequency content over distance, making each repetition warmer and darker.

Reverb

Reverb (reverberation) simulates the way sound reflects off surfaces in a physical space. A small room has short, dense reflections; a cathedral has long, diffuse ones. Reverb is perhaps the single most important effect for making synthesized sounds feel "real" and "present."

open example in new window

reverb.process(source, reverbTime, decayRate): reverbTime is how long the reverb tail lasts (in seconds), and decayRate controls how quickly it fades (higher = faster decay). A reverbTime of 0.5 simulates a small room; 5+ simulates a cathedral or concert hall.

Combining Effects

Real sound design chains multiple effects together. A common creative chain for generative ambient music:

// oscillator → filter → delay → reverb → speakers
osc.disconnect();
osc.connect(filt);

filt.disconnect();
filt.connect(delay);

delay.disconnect();
delay.connect(reverb);

// The reverb output goes to speakers by default

Each stage transforms the sound. The filter shapes the timbre, the delay creates rhythmic echoes, and the reverb places everything in a virtual space. Changing any one parameter: the filter cutoff, the delay time, the reverb length: can completely transform the character of the output.
